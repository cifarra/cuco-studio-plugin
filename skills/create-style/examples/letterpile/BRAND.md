# Letterpile — style from a single style frame

## Brief (context)

**Source.** One style frame (`assets/images/styleframe.png`, 1698×1700): a square
poster of oversized capital letters (M O O G L O N) piled on a sunflower-yellow field.
No brand, no copy. The frame is the whole brief.

**Image language.**
- Flat, opaque colour fills, no gradients, no light, no texture. Every letter is a
  separate cut-out object.
- A uniform warm-black outline, ~9 px at 1700 px, the SAME weight on every letter
  whatever its size: a line weight, not a proportional stroke.
- Letters overlap in a painter's order. A later letter's fill hides the earlier
  letter's fill AND outline, so the pile has real depth with no shadows.
- One letter (the big G) is nearly the field colour: only its outline separates it.
- Letters are upright, huge and cropped by the frame edge. Scale varies wildly (the M
  is 4× the N). One letter is doubled: a blue L sits on a green L, offset down-left.
- Mood: Swiss grotesk meets a children's alphabet set (magnetic fridge letters, foam
  bath letters). Playful, loud, confident, legible from across a room.

**Palette (sampled from pixels).**

| Role | Hex | Use in the frame |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Dominant field | `#FADF4F` sunflower | ~32% + the near-field G |
| 2 Ink | `#1A0E0B` warm black | every outline |
| 3 Accent | `#EA3323` signal red | one letter (the O) |
| Letter set | `#B9CDEB` periwinkle · `#4BA5D9` sky · `#F3DA5A` pale yellow · `#469D5C` green · `#6A85D9` cornflower · `#F19CCF` pink · `#DEDBCF` cream | one colour per letter |

**Type.** A bold neo-grotesk: vertical-stemmed M, round O, G with a spur.
**Helvetica Neue Bold** (`HelveticaNeue-Bold`) was the closest of 13 installed
candidates in a rendered specimen. All caps.

## World (design)

**Material + process.** A pile of chunky cut-out letters on a yellow table. Words are
built by STACKING letters onto the pile: each new letter lands on top of whatever is
there, hiding it.

**Empty frame.** Yellow table, a pile of 2–5 giant cropped letters taken from the
story's own words, outlined, overlapping. The pile is built from the story, so it
changes with every section and grows as the video goes on.

**Signature verb: STACK.** Letters drop onto the pile one at a time and land with
weight (a short overshoot). They leave by being lifted off in reverse order
(unstack), or swept, rolled or tipped by a bigger letter. Occlusion IS the motion: a
landing letter covers what was there.

**Type in the world.** The type IS the material. Each phrase is a row of cut-out
letters: one colour per letter from the letter set, the uniform ink outline, tracking
tight enough that outlines overlap. Later letters stack on earlier ones. Giant
letters from the same words make the ground the phrase lands on.

**Palette use.**
- Yellow is the table; ink draws every edge.
- Red is spent once per composition, on the anchor letter (like the frame's one red
  O).
- Phrase letters cycle the other seven colours, offset per phrase so no two phrases
  share a colour pattern.

**Media becomes material.** Photos are flattened into the same world: posterised into
flat bands mapped onto the letter palette, with ink contours. They appear only inside
letters: in the counter of a giant O (a porthole), or seen through a giant letter
used as a window, or framed by giant letters crowding in from the edges.

**Trinity (wired in pass 2).**
- FORM: from literal (clean cut-outs, photo nearly original) to abstract (photo fully
  flattened, letters scaled wildly).
- FLOW: drop height and tumble; a pile that settles vs one that keeps shifting.
- PRESSURE: overlap density and jitter; neat rows vs a heap.

**Canvas.** 1080×1920 at 30 fps.
